AIROD-21 — Iris recognition under ocular disease

Lead student

FBI / WVU Research Corporation cooperative agreement · May 2021 – Aug 2023 · NIR + visible

Cataracts, glaucoma, and surgical changes alter the iris itself, so a matcher trained on healthy eyes quietly degrades on the population that most needs identifying. I measured that degradation and built systems to work around it: a ResNet-50 matcher on VGGFace2 weights producing 2,048-dimensional embeddings, benchmarked against a commercial SDK and two research algorithms; a manual segmentation tool that removed segmentation error as a confound; GP-GAN synthesis to overcome having only one subject per rare pathology; and a healthy-versus-diseased classifier. All four contract deliverables closed at 100%.

FID-2019 — Face and iris data collection

Lead student

Oct 2019 – Jan 2023 · West Virginia University → University of Georgia · Visible + NIR

A paired face-and-iris collection built subject by subject across two universities and a pandemic that paused it for nearly two years. I ran recruitment, scheduling, equipment, and the collection team, owned IRB consent and compliance including protocol amendments and participant withdrawals, and wrote the final report — including the demographic analysis and a contrast-based image-quality study comparing sensors across capture distances. The dataset now serves as a healthy baseline for other studies, including AIROD-21.

FaceSensor-ID — Which camera took this photo?

First author

IEEE SoutheastCon 2024 · Visible + NIR

Identifying the originating camera sensor from a single face image — a provenance question with direct bearing on image forensics and fraud detection. A transfer-learning system over ResNet50, InceptionV3, EfficientNetB0, and VGG16, cross-validated across five benchmark datasets. Building it also taught me a lesson I now apply by default: early accuracy looked suspiciously high until I rebuilt the splits to be subject-disjoint, the same leakage trap that inflates results in fraud data.
